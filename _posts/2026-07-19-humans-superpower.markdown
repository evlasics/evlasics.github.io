Unfortunately, I'm thinking about AI again. It's hard not to think about if you are a patron of The Atlantic, as half of their pieces seem to be on the topic. I really like their stuff, by the way. I'm no neoliberal&mdash;so I avoid their political articles&mdash;but what stands out as a consistent theme in their work is that they have high standards for life. They are constantly questioning modern life and things which we now take to be normal, which are really symptoms of the current era. Naturally, AI, being one of the most spiritually repugnant inventions ever created, finds itself drawing their ire frequently.

Humans have a couple superpowers which we often take for granted. The most obvious of these is language. Speaking on its own is probably one of the greatest things we ever devised, but this is natural to humans; it's in our DNA. Reading and writing are not. They are powers we had to develop on our own. Whereas speaking grants one the ability to transmit one's thoughts to another, writing grants one the ability to crystallize one's thoughts, and reading is the act of incorporating these ossified stores of knowledge into your own mind. Think about how it feels to read. When you read, you imagine a voice (allegedly some people don't do this, so if you are one of them, skip this section). It may not be the author's or narrators' or your own voice, but a voice for sure. By reading text, especially text with meaning, we anthropomorphize it. We turn the text into its own virtual being when we read, who confers information to us. Indeed, you are probably doing this right now. So for something entirely inhuman to insert itself into this process is offensive to those of us with flesh, blood, and souls. I am not a spiritual person; in fact I am a strong atheist, in the sense that I believe that God does not exist (unless your definition of God is entirely different from what most people mean&mdash;unless you are Spinoza or something) rather than being agnostic on the matter. My references to souls and spirits are not paranormal; your soul is as real as money is. I can go on about the further offence caused by them interloping in art and such, but all this has been said before.

My current focus is to discuss why current AI sucks at doing anything good. It's because it has no will. What do I mean by this? Let's suppose you ask an AI to solve a math problem. The AI will then attempt to solve the math problem, and continue until it makes a mistake or solves it. This is not what a human will do. A human will try for a very short time, then give up and do something different, potentially after cursing you out for giving them such a hard problem. The superpower therefore is laziness. In my research, I've often been asked by my professor &ldquo;do this really hard thing&rdquo; to which I go home, attempt it, it's not working out super well, then I try a different approach. AI will never say &ldquo;sorry I got stuck with your prompt, so I went off on my own and did something completely different instead&rdquo; and if it did do this it would piss users off. But this sort of thing is how we make progress. Because I didn't spend my time reading through the entirety of the paper my professor told me to read (it was 100 pages long and the crucial change of variables didn't work in our case), I developed a new route to prove a part of our theorem, and therefore we made more progress than if I had just listened to the professor. To be clear, the professor had hinted at this approach and had remarked that the paper might not be applicable; it was also before he had taken a very close look at the paper himself (he was busy with other stuff) so I don't mean to insinuate he did anything wrong. What I mean to say is that some requests are unreasonable because we are not omnipotent. If I ask an AI &ldquo;prove this theorem&rdquo; then it will probably spend a lot of time looking into a proof before it starts testing whether counterexamples exist. Or at least, longer than a human would.

Laziness is how many useful tools in mathematics are invented. Why don't we embed manifolds in Euclidean space? Because writing things out in coordinates gets tedious. Why do we use Einstein summation conventions? Because the summation signs get annoying otherwise. Why do we have so many rich tools of analysis? Because physicists were too lazy to do their math properly, so they just left it to the mathematicians to pick up after them. AI doesn't care about how much effort it's going to have to expend (and one can speculate that potentially AI *wants* to expend effort based on which companies provide these AI models) so it will just compute for years on end. This is why AI-formalized proofs suck as well. AI isn't lazy enough to make something a human could understand.
